# Pricing Recommendation · Roamly Groups

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Grow revenue from a known audience

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based
- **Who pays, and for what:** Each friend pays for their own place, plus a small group-booking fee on their own share, at the moment they join the booking. The organiser pays only their own share, so nobody fronts the money or chases anyone. Hosts keep paying Roamly's usual commission on each booking, unchanged. A transaction fee fits because value arrives once per completed group booking, not continuously, so a subscription would charge groups who book only a few times a year.

## 3. Packaging

- **Tiers:**
  - Good: Groups Basic, for small groups of 2–3 friends trying it for the first time. No extra fee; each friend pays their own share. Rough price: £0 on top of the experience.
  - Better : Groups, for the organiser booking a vetted experience for 3–6 friends (our core audience). Split payments before booking, shared itinerary, and drop-out options (go ahead with fewer, cover a share, invite a replacement). Rough price: £2 per person per booking.
  - Best: Groups Plus, for larger or special-occasion groups (7+ people, birthdays, hen and stag parties). Everything in Groups, plus private experiences, flexible cancellation and priority support. Rough price: 5% of each person's share.

## 4. Assumption to test

- **The one assumption I would test before launch:** That invited friends will pay their share plus a small fee before the booking is confirmed, instead of dropping out. If friends refuse to pay upfront, or the fee makes them hesitate, the group stalls and the organiser is left chasing people again, which breaks our core promise and the whole transaction model. I would test it in the pilot by measuring how many invited friends pay within 24 hours, with and without the fee.

## 5. One-page recommendation

### Pricing Recommendation: Roamly Groups

**To:** Finance, Sales Leadership, Product Marketing
**Status:** V1 draft. All prices are illustrative assumptions to be tested.

#### Recommendation in one line
A transaction-based group fee: free for pairs, **£2 per person per booking for groups of 3–6 (recommended tier)**, and 5% per person for groups of 7+. Each friend pays their own share plus the fee when they join. Host commission is unchanged.

#### Goal
**Grow revenue from a known audience.** Our bet targets existing Roamly customers who already try to book with friends but drop off. We earn by turning attempted group bookings into completed ones, not by winning a new market.

#### Why transaction-based
- **Value arrives once per completed group booking**, not continuously. A subscription would charge groups who book only a few times a year.
- **The fee is split across everyone**, so the organiser never fronts the money or chases anyone. This protects our positioning: *plan the experience, not the payback.*
- **Hosts keep paying the usual commission**, so supply-side economics don't change.

#### Packaging (Good-Better-Best)

| Tier | For | Includes | Rough price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Groups Basic** | Pairs trying it for the first time | Each person pays their own share | £0 extra |
| **Groups ★ (recommended)** | Organisers booking a vetted experience for 3–6 friends: our core audience | Split payments before booking, shared itinerary, drop-out options (go ahead with fewer, cover a share, invite a replacement) | £2 per person per booking |
| **Groups Plus** | Groups of 7+ and special occasions (birthdays, hen and stag parties) | Everything in Groups, plus private experiences, flexible cancellation, priority support | 5% of each person's share |

**Natural upgrade:** the fee only starts once a real group (3+) completes a booking, when value is highest.

#### What each stakeholder needs to hear

**Finance: "It adds revenue without new risk."**
- New fee revenue on top of unchanged host commission.
- Revenue grows only when bookings complete, matching our success metric: a proposed 15% rise in group booking completion within 90 days.
- To confirm: payment processing costs per transaction, and whether a £2 fee covers them.

**Sales Leadership: "It is easy to explain and removes the main objection."**
- One simple rule: the fee depends on group size.
- It answers the organiser's biggest objection to group bookings: "I'll end up paying for everyone."
- Groups Plus gives a clear offer for larger, higher-value occasions.

**Product Marketing: "The price supports the promise."**
- The fee is per person and paid at joining, so no one becomes the "debt collector."
- Lead with the value, not the fee: *Everyone pays their own share before you book.*
- Show the fee clearly at checkout so it never feels hidden.

#### Risks and trade-offs
- **Fee resistance:** invited friends may hesitate at an extra charge.
- **Competitive gap:** groups already trust Airbnb and use WhatsApp and Venmo for free.
- **Cannibalisation:** pairs may stay on the free tier.

#### Rollout
- Launch to pilot customers first, with the fee shown at checkout from day one.
- Set a rollback threshold before launch, for example if invited-friend payment completion falls below an agreed level.
- Monitor: payment completion within 24 hours, booking completion, drop-outs and support tickets.

#### Assumption to test before launch
**Invited friends will pay their share plus a small fee before the booking is confirmed, instead of dropping out.** If they won't, the group stalls, the organiser is left chasing people again, and both our promise and the model break.

**Best research method: Conjoint analysis.** The question is how friends trade price against what they get: fee or no fee, fee level, paying upfront, and drop-out flexibility. Conjoint tests whole packages at different prices, so it shows whether the fee changes the decision to join and what each feature is worth. Van Westendorp only gives a price range, and Max-Diff only ranks features without prices. Confirm the result with a live pilot A/B test: friends invited with the fee vs without, measuring payment within 24 hours.
