# Messaging Pillars · Roamly Groups

## 1. From/To market shift

- **From, life before:** The friend who ends up organising is stuck sorting out the plan and chasing people for money. They coordinate in WhatsApp, book on their own card through platforms like Airbnb, then chase everyone on Venmo to get paid back. When someone drops out, the organiser absorbs the cost or sorts it out alone, and some groups give up on doing things together.
- **To, life after:** The group agrees, books and pays in one shared plan, with each friend paying their own share before anything is booked. Nobody is left out of pocket, plans can change without one person absorbing the cost, and the organiser gets to enjoy the experience instead of managing it.

## 2. Audiences

- **Number of audiences that need their own message:** 2
- **Why you picked this number:** The organiser and the invited friends care about fundamentally different outcomes. The organiser chooses Roamly and carries the risk of planning, paying upfront and chasing money. The invited friends did not choose the product; they just click a link and need paying their share to be quick, clear and trustworthy. One message would either miss the organiser's pain or ask friends to care about planning they are not doing.

## 3. Messaging pillars

### Audience 1: The friend who ends up organising a local experience for a group of 3–6, usually an existing Roamly customer who books on their own card and chases everyone for money.
- **Value prop:** Plan the experience, not the payback. With one shared plan and each friend paying their own share, you no longer have to manage everyone or chase payments.
- **Capabilities:** Shared itinerary where the group chooses from vetted local experiences; one invite link for the whole group; each friend pays their own share before the booking is confirmed; options to continue with fewer people, cover a share or invite a replacement if someone drops out.
- **Evidence:** Pilot targets, to be validated: organisers spend 0 hours chasing payments, compared with an estimated 2 hours per trip today; group booking completion rises 15% on baseline within 90 days; 30% of pilot organisers book another group experience within 6 months. Target pilot quote: "I booked the experience and didn't have to chase anyone for money afterwards.

### Audience 2: The invited friend: someone in the group who receives the organiser's link and needs to join and pay their share, and may not have a Roamly account.
- **Value prop:** You're in, your share is paid, and you're not the one holding the group up.
- **Capabilities:** Join from a single link; see the plan and your exact share upfront; pay in seconds without needing to set up a full account first; clear updates on who has paid and when the booking is confirmed.
- **Evidence:** Pilot targets, to be validated: 80% of invited friends pay their share within 24 hours; paying takes under 30 seconds from opening the link; 70% of invited friends accept the invitation; fewer than 2 payment support issues per 100 group bookings.

## 4. Copy across three surfaces

- **Homepage headline:** Plan the experience, not the payback.
Book vetted local experiences with your friends, and everyone pays their own share before you book.

Leads with: the value proposition.
Why: A homepage visitor decides in seconds whether this is for them, so it opens with the outcome the organiser wants most: not fronting the money or chasing friends. The second line adds two capabilities, vetted local experiences and paying your own share, as proof and to make it specific to Roamly.
- **Social post:** Planned the group activity. Paid for everyone. Spent the next week chasing friends for money. 

Not anymore. With Roamly Groups, your friends pick a vetted local experience together and each pays their own share before anything is booked. Someone drops out? Go ahead with fewer people or invite someone else.

Be the friend who plans it, not the one who chases the money.

Leads with: the "From" pain, then the value proposition, with capabilities as proof.
Why: Social posts need to stop people scrolling. Describing a moment most organisers recognise makes them feel seen, and shareable, before introducing the product. The drop-out option answers the next worry they would have.
- **Email subject line:** You shouldn't have to be the group's debt collector

Leads with: the "From" pain.
Why: A subject line has one job: getting the email opened. Naming the organiser's frustration in their own words ("debt collector") is more likely to catch attention in a busy inbox than describing a feature. The email itself can then deliver the value proposition and capabilities.
