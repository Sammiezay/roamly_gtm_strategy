# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet · Roamly Groups

## 1. Primary competitor

- **Competitor:** Airbnb (Airbnb Experiences)
- **Core offering:** A travel marketplace offering stays and hosted experiences. Travellers can find accommodation and things to do on the same platform.
- **How they position themselves:** A trusted, one-stop place for travel, where guests can book both where they stay and what they do through hosts on the platform.
- **Primary audience:** Leisure travellers, including friends and families, especially those already booking accommodation on Airbnb.
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Airbnb typically charges a 20% service fee on experience reservations. It launched split payments for eligible accommodation bookings in 2017.
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** Friends travelling together may already be on Airbnb to book their stay. They already trust the brand and have an account, so adding an experience there is easier than trying a new platform. If Airbnb extended group coordination and split payments to experiences, it could weaken our main differentiator. Our opening is to be the best product for coordinating a group around vetted local experiences, rather than an add-on to accommodation.

## 3. What I want to revisit in my Module 1 strategy

The one thing I would adjust is my value proposition. My V1 line, "Plan together, pay separately and book as one group", describes features. The battlecard shows that coordinated booking, split payments and shared itineraries are not defensible on their own because Airbnb could build them.

It also made me realise that my strategy focuses on improving Roamly's own booking journey, but does not explain why a group should choose Roamly over Airbnb or the status quo of WhatsApp and Venmo.

I would reposition around the insight already in my research: one organizer carries the work of getting everyone to agree, book and pay. My updated value proposition is: "Roamly Groups helps friends agree on and book vetted local experiences together, without one person having to organize everything or chase payments, even when plans change."

The features and flexible participant options then become proof of that promise, while vetted local experiences and a focus on groups give us a harder-to-copy advantage. I would keep Expand as the goal and group booking completion as the primary KPI, but use organizer interviews and the pilot to test this message, including what groups would otherwise use.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** I am doubling down on my Module 1 segment: groups of friends travelling together. Specifically, Roamly Groups is for the person organising a local experience for 3–6 friends, starting with existing Roamly customers who attempted but did not complete a group booking in the previous 90 days. The battlecard did not reveal a better segment, but it showed that the organiser is the person I need to win.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** It removes the organiser's burden when booking a vetted local experience: the group can agree, book and pay together, so one person does not have to manage everything and chase payments. This still needs validating with customers.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Airbnb could extend group coordination and split payments to experiences. Friends may already be using it for their stay, trust the brand and have accounts, so adding an experience there would be easier than trying Roamly. If that happened, our features alone would not protect us.

### Insight
The signal is that customers are already coordinating bookings across separate accounts, splitting payments manually and synchronising availability themselves. This suggests the demand exists, and the barrier may be the burden on the organizer rather than a lack of interest. Airbnb wins through habit, trust and convenience, so Roamly Groups cannot take that buyer by simply adding similar features. It must make booking a group experience clearly easier for the organiser and use vetted local experiences as a reason to choose Roamly.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when an organiser wants a shared local experience but does not want to carry the work of getting everyone to agree, book and pay, because that burden pushes groups to abandon bookings or fall back on WhatsApp and Venmo.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by becoming the easiest way for existing customers to book a vetted local experience with 3–6 friends. For the next 90 days, we will focus on shared planning, split payments and options for when plans change, and measure success through group booking completion, with a proposed 15% relative increase on baseline.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing corporate teams, families and other segments; accommodation or an all-in-one trip-planning product; broad acquisition campaigns; and large-scale community programmes until product-led adoption is validated. I am also choosing not to match Airbnb's scale or compete on features alone.
