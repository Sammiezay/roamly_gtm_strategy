# GTM Strategy · Roamly Groups

## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** Roamly is losing potential group bookings because its existing platform does not support seamless group coordination, split payments and shared planning. This creates friction, increases booking abandonment and limits opportunities to grow customer adoption and engagement.
- **What success looks like:** Success means increased group booking completion, reduced drop-off rates and strong adoption of Roamly Groups. Customers can organise and pay for shared experiences easily, improving satisfaction, loyalty and repeat usage. The launch also strengthens Roamly's reputation as a convenient platform for group travel and attracts new customers through invitations and referrals.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** For Roamly, the strongest signal is behavioural data pointing to unmet demand. Customers are already trying to organise group bookings, even though the platform doesn't properly support them.

This is important because customers are demonstrating demand through their actions, not simply saying they might want a feature.
Customer support reports show that users are coordinating bookings across separate accounts, splitting payments manually and synchronising availability themselves. High drop-off rates indicate friction in the booking process. These behaviours demonstrate existing unmet demand for group booking functionality, while Roamly's strong retention and established host network provide a foundation for expansion.
- **Top 5 possible segments:** Groups of friends travelling together: Travellers who want to coordinate shared experiences, manage bookings and split payments conveniently.
Families travelling together: Families who need to organise activities and coordinate bookings for multiple family members.
Couples travelling with other couples: Travellers looking to plan joint experiences while managing individual payments.
Corporate teams and retreat organisers: Businesses arranging group activities, team-building experiences and shared itineraries.
Travel communities and group organisers: Individuals organising shared travel experiences for groups with common interests.
- **How to size the opportunity:** I would assess the number of customers attempting group bookings, booking abandonment rates, average group sizes, booking frequency and potential booking value. I would analyse Roamly's internal booking data, customer support records, user feedback and product analytics to understand existing demand. I would also conduct customer interviews, surveys, competitor analysis and external market research to estimate the potential market size, identify underserved customer segments and assess the commercial viability of Roamly Groups.

Customer Support and UX Research would provide insights into customer pain points and unmet needs, while Data Analytics would validate demand and measure performance. ## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on

Here is my pushback on one of your assumptions: Assumption 2: One participant failing to pay will automatically result in group booking abandonment.

 I would challenge the assumption that one participant failing to complete payment would automatically lead to booking abandonment. Group bookings involve shared decision-making, and customers may have alternative ways to proceed.
For example, if five friends plan an experience and one cannot pay, the remaining four could choose to continue with a smaller group, redistribute the outstanding cost or invite another friend to replace the original participant.
Rather than treating incomplete individual payments as an automatic barrier to conversion, I would explore how Roamly Groups could accommodate these scenarios through flexible participant management, payment adjustments and replacement invitations, subject to host availability and booking policies.
I would validate these behaviours through customer interviews and a controlled pilot, measuring how frequently groups successfully complete bookings after a participant withdraws or fails to pay.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

### Roamly Groups | V1 Go-to-Market Strategy

**Product:** Roamly Groups  
**Primary Goal:** Expand  
**GTM Motion:** Hybrid – Product-Led Growth (PLG) + Community-Led Growth (CLG)  
**Launch Approach:** 90-day phased rollout  
**Status:** V1 Draft

#### 1. Business Opportunity

Roamly is losing potential group bookings because customers must coordinate reservations, payments and availability across separate accounts. Existing workarounds and high booking drop-off indicate unmet demand for a simpler group booking experience.

**Objective:** Increase completed group bookings among existing customers by reducing coordination and payment friction.

#### 2. Target Customer Segment

**Primary Audience:** Existing Roamly customers organising local experiences for groups of 3–6 friends.

**Initial Pilot Audience:** Customers who attempted but did not complete a group booking within the previous 90 days, where identifiable through booking analytics.

**Core Customer Need:** Organise shared experiences without managing separate bookings, chasing individual payments or coordinating plans across multiple platforms.

#### 3. Value Proposition

> Plan together, pay separately and book as one group, with the flexibility to adjust participants and payments when plans change.

Roamly Groups will provide coordinated bookings, split payments and shared itinerary planning.

The pilot will also explore three potential options when a participant withdraws or fails to pay:

- Proceed with fewer participants.
- Allow another participant to cover the outstanding payment.
- Invite a replacement participant.

These options remain proposed capabilities, subject to customer validation, technical feasibility and host booking policies.

#### 4. Recommended GTM Motion and Channel Approach

##### Primary: Product-Led Growth (PLG)

**Goal:** Drive feature adoption and increase completed bookings among existing Roamly customers.

- **In-app prompts:** Introduce Roamly Groups during experience selection and checkout.
- **Targeted email campaigns:** Re-engage customers who previously abandoned group booking attempts, where identifiable and contactable.
- **Group invitation links:** Enable organisers to invite friends directly into a shared booking journey.

##### Secondary: Community-Led Growth (CLG)

**Goal:** Build awareness, trust and organic adoption through existing travel communities.

- Partner with 5 selected travel community organisers for an initial pilot.
- Demonstrate group booking experiences through travel communities and social media.
- Use tracked invitation links to measure community-driven adoption.

**Channel Priority:** Validate product-led adoption first, then scale community engagement based on performance.

#### 5. Launch Plan

| Phase | Timeline | Key Activities |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | Weeks 1–2 | Establish baseline metrics, identify pilot customers and confirm host/payment readiness |
| Controlled Pilot | Weeks 3–6 | Launch to eligible customers, monitor bookings and test payment scenarios |
| Optimisation | Weeks 7–10 | Analyse drop-offs, improve booking experience and test community channels |
| Evaluation | Weeks 11–13 | Assess performance and decide whether to scale, iterate or pause |

#### 6. Success Metrics

##### Primary KPI: Group Booking Completion Rate

**Definition:** Percentage of initiated group bookings that result in confirmed reservations.

**Proposed Target:** Achieve a **15% relative increase in group booking completion rate within 90 days**, compared with the pre-launch baseline.

##### Supporting Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| Feature Adoption Rate | Percentage of eligible customers initiating Roamly Groups bookings |
| Invitation Acceptance Rate | Percentage of invited participants joining a group booking |
| Payment Completion Rate | Percentage of invited participants successfully completing payments |
| Booking Recovery Rate | Percentage of affected group bookings completed after a participant withdraws or fails to pay |
| Cancellation Rate | Percentage of confirmed group bookings subsequently cancelled |
| Payment Support Incidents | Number of payment-related support issues per 100 group booking attempts |

#### 7. Key Hypothesis and Validation

**Hypothesis:** Providing coordinated group bookings, split payments and appropriate flexibility when group arrangements change will reduce booking abandonment and increase completed bookings.

**Validation Approach:**

- Conduct customer interviews to understand how groups respond when a participant withdraws.
- Run a controlled pilot to observe booking and payment behaviours.
- Measure how frequently groups continue with fewer participants, redistribute payments or invite replacements.
- Compare group booking completion against the pre-launch baseline, using a comparable customer cohort.

**Key Risk:** Host booking policies, availability constraints and payment-system limitations may restrict flexibility.

**Mitigation:** Validate these constraints with Engineering, Finance, Legal and Host Partnerships before implementing additional functionality.

#### 8. Launch Decision Criteria

At the end of the 90-day rollout:

- **Scale:** If group booking completion improves by at least 15% relative to baseline without material deterioration in cancellations, payment reliability or support demand.
- **Iterate:** If customers adopt the feature but continue abandoning bookings at specific stages.
- **Pause:** If payment reliability, host availability or customer experience presents significant unresolved risks.

---

#### Strategic Recommendation

Prioritise existing Roamly customers who demonstrate group booking intent. Use PLG to reduce booking friction and drive adoption, supported by targeted community engagement.

Validate flexible payment and participant-management behaviours before expanding the feature or committing to broader acquisition campaigns.

**Success means more completed group bookings, fewer abandoned transactions and a more convenient shared travel experience.**

*Note: The 15% improvement target, 3–6-person segment, five community partners and 90-day rollout are proposed V1 planning assumptions requiring validation against Roamly's actual customer and commercial data.*
